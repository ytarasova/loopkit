## Problem

What is wrong today, for whom, how often, and what it costs. Two to four
sentences. Cite where the evidence came from (ticket, support thread,
metric, code).

## Proposed outcome

What a person will see or be able to do that they cannot today. State it as
observable behaviour, not as a mechanism.

## Affected users and systems

Roles and rough numbers. Services, repos, teams, external parties.

## Constraints

What must not change, and who set that. Deadlines with their reason. Limits
that exist in the code today (rate limits, quotas, policies) that the outcome
has to live within.

## Proof

What would show it is done: items, each with its
verifications and who makes each one. Each verification passes or fails (a
test, a build, a screenshot, a metric) and keeps the output it gave. Ends
with the riskiest assumption and what would show it false.

## Open questions

Each with who can answer it. An open question is not a blocker to planning;
it is a marker the planner must carry.
